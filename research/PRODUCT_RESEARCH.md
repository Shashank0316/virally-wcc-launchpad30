# Virally Product Research

Date: 4 October 2026
Primary track: Agentic AI
Product: Virally

## 1. Research objective

Validate the product problem, understand how the market currently approaches audience and concept validation, identify a narrow product gap, and translate those findings into product requirements for Virally.

## 2. Why this problem was selected

The WCC Launchpad 30 brief explicitly assigns 15 judging points to user insight and problem evidence and asks teams to show that the problem is real. The event also says the core task is to build one focused workflow around a problem worth solving.

Virally therefore targets a specific pre launch decision: a founder or marketer has a business idea and a piece of marketing creative, but does not yet know how different audience segments might react before distribution spend is committed.

## 3. Research method

### Secondary research
Reviewed the official WCC event brief plus current official product documentation for adjacent tools in product discovery, synthetic research and population simulation.

### Workflow mapping
Mapped the current decision journey as: idea → creative → informal feedback or generic benchmarks → launch spend → observe reach and engagement → iterate. The opportunity is to move a directional audience screen earlier in that loop.

### Competitive framing
Compared four adjacent products: Maze, Delve AI, Simile and Aaru. These tools validate that AI personas, synthetic respondents and population simulation are active product categories. Virally narrows the use case to pre launch marketing creative and social reaction behavior.

### Product translation
Research findings were translated into explicit persona traits, action probabilities, trend context, decomposable score drivers, a research evidence view and a strong human validation disclaimer.

## 4. Key findings

1. The hackathon itself makes problem evidence a first class judging criterion.
2. The market already contains tools for participant based research, synthetic user research and large scale behavioral simulation.
3. The product opportunity for Virally is the specific social reaction question: what different audience types would likely do with this idea or creative — like, comment, share, save, skip or show purchase intent?
4. Synthetic results need transparency. The interface should expose assumptions, persona differences, trend context and confidence instead of presenting an unexplained prediction.
5. Real user interviews, surveys or launch data remain necessary for production decisions.

## 5. Competitive landscape

### Maze — product discovery
Maze describes itself as a continuous product discovery platform supporting moderated and unmoderated product research, participant recruitment, reporting and sharing.
Source: https://help.maze.co/articles/4777475916-maze-quick-start

### Delve AI — synthetic market research
Delve AI describes research backed personas, synthetic users, audience segmentation, surveys, interviews and concept or message validation. It also emphasizes updating personas as new research data arrives.
Source: https://www.delve.ai/solutions/market-research

### Simile — behavior simulation
Simile describes AI driven simulations for testing how customers, employees or populations respond to change. It emphasizes grounding populations in real people and validating simulations against real human behavior.
Source: https://www.simile.com/

### Aaru — population simulation
Aaru describes simulations built around explicit objectives, audiences, conditions and outcomes, with separate evaluation and validation practices and clear limits on what a simulation can claim.
Source: https://aaru.com/simulation

## 6. Differentiation hypothesis

Virally is not trying to replace full market research. It is positioned as a fast pre launch creative screening layer for founders, marketers, creators and small teams. Its distinctive interaction is a visible room of disagreeing synthetic audience agents whose reactions are aggregated into a transparent decision signal.

## 7. Product decisions derived from research

1. Multiple audience personas instead of one generic AI opinion.
2. Explicit behavioral dimensions: novelty, social behavior, price sensitivity, trust, utility and visual preference.
3. Separate social actions: Like, Comment, Share, Save and Skip.
4. Purchase intent as a separate conversion signal rather than blending it into engagement.
5. Trend momentum as context that can shift receptivity over time.
6. Dashboard drill down so every topline score can be inspected.
7. Research evidence page so the problem, sources, differentiation and limitations stay visible.
8. Human in the loop disclaimer and validation workflow.

## 8. What this research does not prove

This is secondary and methodological research conducted for the hackathon prototype. It does not prove that real customers will behave exactly like the simulated personas. No interviews, testimonials or survey results are fabricated in this repository. The next validation step is to collect primary responses from actual founders, marketers or creators and compare those needs with the current product assumptions.

## 9. Primary validation questions

1. How do you currently test a new idea or marketing concept before launch?
2. What is the most expensive uncertainty in that process?
3. How many people or data points do you normally get before deciding to launch?
4. Would a fast simulated audience screen change what you launch or how you market it?
5. Which audience reaction would be most useful to see before spending on distribution?
